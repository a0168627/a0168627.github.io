🧭 What mtb-survey Does

Most trail maps only show you where a path goes. They do not tell you whether it is a relaxed forest road or a steep, rocky chute that will have you walking. mtb-survey fixes that by letting you record trail difficulty while you are actually riding, using the international mtb:scale rating that OpenStreetMap understands.

The workflow is simple from beginning to end:

  1. You ride with your Garmin watch or Garmin Edge and mark difficulty as you go.
  2. The ride is saved as a normal FIT file, the same format your device already produces.
  3. mtb-survey on your Windows computer reads that file and turns it into an interactive map.
  4. You review the colours, adjust anything you marked by mistake, and write the result to OpenStreetMap.

Because everything is built around files you already own, you stay in control. Nothing is uploaded until you choose to publish it.

🚀 Your First Survey Ride

You do not need any special setup on the trail. Ride as you normally would, and use your device to note the difficulty of the sections that stand out.

  1. Start a ride on your Garmin watch or Edge exactly as you always do.
  2. Ride the trail. When a section feels noticeably easier or harder than the rest, mark it on the device.
  3. Finish and save the ride when you get back to the car.
  4. Copy the ride file from your Garmin to your Windows computer by plugging the device in with its USB cable and dragging the file across, or by letting your existing Garmin sync tool save it locally.
  5. Open mtb-survey and load the ride file you just copied.

That is the entire field workflow. The heavy lifting happens afterwards, on your computer, where you have a proper screen and a mouse.

🗺️ Turning Your Ride Into a Map

When you load a ride into mtb-survey, the route is drawn on an interactive map you can pan and zoom, with each section coloured by its recorded difficulty. The map is built with Leaflet, so it behaves like the maps you are used to on the web: drag to move, scroll to zoom, click to inspect.

What you see on the map:

  • Your full route, drawn as a continuous line so you can see how the ride fits together.
  • Colour-coded difficulty for every marked section, so easy and hard parts stand out instantly.
  • Section details when you click a line, including the recorded rating and where it sits on the trail.
  • Gaps and unmarked stretches, so you can spot anything you forgot to record.

This review step is where the real value appears. You can compare what you felt on the bike with what actually got recorded, fix anything that looks wrong, and merge small fragments into one sensible trail line before publishing.

🌍 Writing Your Results to OpenStreetMap

OpenStreetMap is the free, open map that many apps and websites use behind the scenes. Adding trail difficulty there means your work helps every rider who uses that data afterwards.

From mtb-survey you can send the finished survey to OpenStreetMap. You will need a free OpenStreetMap account, which takes a minute to create on their website. Once you are signed in, the survey you built is written to the map as trail information tagged with the mtb:scale rating you recorded.

  • Your edits are attributed to your account, as with any OpenStreetMap contribution.
  • You can review changes before they go live.
  • Other mappers can see and improve what you added, which is exactly how a good shared map gets better.
Only publish trails you actually rode and are confident about. Accurate, honest difficulty data is far more useful than a fast, rough survey.

📊 Understanding mtb:scale

The mtb:scale rating runs from 0 to 6. It describes technical difficulty, not fitness. A climb can be exhausting and still be a 0, and a short downhill can be a 4.

RatingWhat it feels like
0Gravel, smooth forest road, or easy path. Almost no technical skill needed.
1Mostly smooth with small roots or stones. Beginner friendly.
2Ruts, loose stones, and tighter corners. Some bike handling required.
3Bigger roots and rocks, steeper sections. Confident intermediate riders.
4Steep, technical, and demanding. Advanced riders only.
5Very steep with large obstacles. Expect to walk parts of it.
6Extreme terrain. Almost entirely unrideable for most people.

If you are unsure between two ratings, choose the lower one. Underrating is easy to correct later; overrating makes the map less trustworthy.

💡 Tips for Better Trail Surveys

  • Ride the trail twice if you can. The second pass is when you notice which sections genuinely stood out.
  • Be consistent. Use the same personal reference point for a "3" every time, or your own map will drift.
  • Mark generously. Small marks are hard to review later; slightly longer marked sections are easier to work with.
  • Check the weather. A trail that is a 2 in the dry can feel like a 3 in the wet. Record what you actually rode.
  • Review the same day. Memory fades fast. Ten minutes on the computer after the ride is worth more than an hour the following week.
  • Keep your ride files. A folder of saved FIT files is a useful archive if you want to revisit an area later.

🛠️ Troubleshooting

The map looks empty after loading a ride

This usually means the ride file has no marked sections. Check that the file you loaded is the finished ride and not an activity summary. Try loading a different ride file to confirm the app is working.

My route is offset from the roads

GPS accuracy under dense tree cover can wander. Give the device a clear view of the sky for a minute before starting your next ride, and the lines will be noticeably cleaner.

Windows will not open the download

Right-click the file, choose Properties, and look for an Unblock checkbox at the bottom of the General tab. Tick it, click Apply, and open the file again.

My OpenStreetMap upload was rejected

Make sure you are signed in to your own OpenStreetMap account and that your edits do not overlap a trail someone else is currently working on. Waiting a few minutes and trying again usually resolves it.

❓ Common Questions

Do I need an internet connection while riding?

No. Your Garmin records everything on the device. You only need a connection on your computer when you are ready to look at the map or publish your survey.

Will this delete the rides on my Garmin?

No. mtb-survey reads a copy of the ride file. Your original activity stays exactly where it is.

Which Garmin devices work?

The app is built for Garmin watches such as the fenix and Forerunner families, and for Garmin Edge bike computers. Any device that saves a standard FIT file will produce a file the desktop app can read.

Do I have to publish to OpenStreetMap?

No. You can use mtb-survey purely to see a colour-coded map of your own ride and never upload anything.

Is it free?

Yes. The application is free to download and use, and OpenStreetMap accounts are free as well.
